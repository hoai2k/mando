# Rig joint audit

Generated by `tools/asset-pipeline/measure-lod.mjs` (with `src/characters/data/lod.json`, and the same data as JSON in `rig-joints.json`); do not edit by hand. No .glb is modified by it.

Every authored character .glb, in its bind pose at the height the game fits it to, with each skeleton joint
compared against where the mesh itself says the joint should be. Offsets are *actual minus estimate*, in cm,
in the model frame (x = the character's left, y up, z forward).

**How the estimate is made.** Limb joints and the neck: the weighted centroid of the vertices whose skin
weights are split between the bones either side of the joint (both >= 0.2) — the seam the skin bends about,
which also sits mid cross-section (`seam`). Where fewer than a dozen vertices are shared, the band of each
side's vertices within 2 cm of the other side stands in (`band`). Spine chain (pelvis, spine, chest): the
centroid of the torso's horizontal cross-section at the bone head; only the horizontal offset counts (`slice`).

**Centre line.** Independently of the estimate, the limb is cut square to its axis at the *actual* joint:
`centre` is how far the joint sits from that section's centre and `radius` the section's mean radius.
A joint further from the centre than the radius is outside the mesh.

**Caveat — read before fixing anything.** The seam estimate inherits the skinning: automatic weights are
computed *from* the bones, so a joint that is misplaced along its limb drags its own seam with it, and the
`along` column will understate it. The `across` and centre-line columns do not have that problem — a bone off
the limb's centre line shows up there whatever the weights did. Robes, capes, armour plates and hair
weighted to a limb widen its sections and pull the centroids; treat any single number as a lead, not a verdict.

**How far to trust it.** Checked by drawing both points on the models: elbows, knees, wrists and ankles
on bare or tight-clad limbs read well — the seam is a clean ring and its centroid sits mid-limb. Shoulders,
hips and the neck are weaker: the seam there is a broad band that takes in pauldrons, collars, the chest and
the pelvis, and under armour or a robe it can sit several centimetres from any sensible pivot. The spine
slices are pulled backward by anything worn on the back (a jetpack moves the chest's centroid by ~10 cm).

Flags: offset over 3 cm; joint more than 35% of the section radius off the centre line; joint outside the mesh.
**Likely misplaced** is only called on the limb joints (elbow, wrist, knee, ankle), where both readings are
trustworthy, and needs them to agree: the seam offset over 4 cm *and* the joint more than half the limb's radius
off its centre line — or the joint outside the mesh outright. The rest are leads for a person to look at.
